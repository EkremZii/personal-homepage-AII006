Chapter II

Understanding Fabrics

Cloth is the language of tailoring. Know when and how to use each fibre.

The Six Essential Cloths

Each fabric has a season, a purpose, and a proper place in a professional wardrobe.

Year-round foundation

Wool

The backbone of tailoring. Tropical wool (7–9 oz) for warm months; flannel and tweed (12–14 oz) for autumn and winter. Always prefer pure wool over poly blends.

Spring & summer

Cotton

Ideal for shirts, chinos, and unstructured jackets. Breathable and easy to care for, but wrinkles more than wool. Oxford cloth for shirts; gabardine for trousers.

High summer

Linen

Unmatched breathability for hot climates. Embrace the wrinkles — they are part of linen's character. Best for suits, shirts, and relaxed summer events.

Evening & accents

Silk

Used in ties, pocket squares, and lining. Adds lustre without weight. A silk-wool blend tie drapes better than pure polyester and ages gracefully.

Cold weather luxury

Cashmere

Reserved for overcoats, knitwear, and scarves. Incredibly soft but delicate — dry clean only, store folded with cedar. Never wear daily; rotate to preserve fibres.

Autumn & countryside

Tweed & Herringbone

Textured wool patterns that add depth without colour. Perfect for sport coats, country events, and layering. Pair with flannel trousers in winter.

How to Choose

  • Hold the fabric to light — a tight weave indicates durability
  • Check the super number (Super 100–130 is ideal for daily wear)
  • Higher super counts feel softer but wear faster
  • Natural fibres breathe; synthetics trap heat and shine under light
  • When in doubt, choose a mid-grey wool — it works in every season
