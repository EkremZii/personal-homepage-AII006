Survey Method

Participants included university classmates, family members, coworkers from our family business, and friends. The group included 14 people with university-level education and 6 high school graduates.

Comments & Conclusions

E-commerce adoption in my survey depends heavily on education. While 75% of all respondents have purchased online, university-educated participants shop online at a dramatically higher rate (93%) than high school graduates (33%). This gap was the clearest pattern in the data.

Key Findings

  • Education is the strongest factor: 13 of 14 university-level respondents (93%) have purchased online, close to the expected 95% rate. Only 2 of 6 high school graduates (33%) have purchased, matching the roughly 30% target. Pew Research has long noted that Americans with higher education and income levels are significantly more likely to shop online.
  • High school group avoids e-commerce: Among the 6 high school graduates, 2 purchased, 2 browsed without buying, and 2 never shopped online at all. This suggests lower digital confidence or less access to payment and delivery options in that group.
  • Young adults dominate: 9 of 20 respondents were ages 18–25, and this group reported the highest weekly purchase rates. Pew's 2022 survey found that 91% of adults ages 18–49 buy things online using a smartphone.
  • Monthly shopping is the norm: Among online buyers, 53% shop about once a month and 33% shop weekly. eMarketer reports that 46% of U.S. consumers make an online purchase at least once per week.
  • Gender is balanced: Male and female respondents were split evenly (10 each), and both groups purchased online at similar rates. Pew data similarly shows modest gender differences in online shopping behavior.
  • Regional favorites matter: Trendyol and Amazon were the top sites, reflecting the mix of local Turkish-market platforms and global retailers available in TRNC.

Industry Context

My survey results align with broader e-commerce growth. The U.S. Census Bureau reported that e-commerce accounted for 16.9% of total U.S. retail sales in Q1 2026, up from roughly 10% a decade earlier. eMarketer forecasts that U.S. retail e-commerce sales exceed $1 trillion annually, with mobile devices driving an increasing share of transactions. The COVID-19 pandemic permanently accelerated online adoption, and post-pandemic data from Pew shows that e-commerce's share of retail has remained well above pre-2020 levels.

In conclusion, e-commerce is mainstream among university-educated adults but far less common among high school graduates. Education level appears to be a better predictor of online shopping than age or gender in this sample. As digital literacy programs expand and payment options become simpler, high school-educated consumers may close this gap over time.
